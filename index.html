What I do

Written in the first person, because a CV already exists.

A concept usually arrives as a mechanism — a pair of foils that should flap on their own, a tail that should make thrust the way a fish's does. My job is to turn it into something measurable: a rig that constrains the motion I care about, load cells that catch the forces in every direction, and a procedure repeatable enough that the numbers still mean something a year later.

Two problems keep coming back. Fully passive systems are harder to characterise than driven ones — nothing imposes the motion, so the operating point emerges from the fluid–structure coupling and you have to measure your way to it rather than set it. And the loads nobody sizes for are often the ones that matter: in our yaw-misalignment tests the flow-perpendicular load turned out to be large and strongly periodic, which is not how a conventional rotary turbine behaves.

A third strand grew out of the first two. If a test result is going to be used to compare two vehicles, the procedure has to be written down, not remembered. I now spend part of my time drafting measurement procedures and group standards for marine unmanned systems.

Research lines

Three strands, one facility.

Fully passive flapping-foil turbines

Self-starting behaviour, bidirectional operation, power transmission, and the system loads that come with non-rotary motion.

Biomimetic caudal-fin propulsion

Multi-DOF tail kinematics, thrust generation, and the yaw recoil an underwater vehicle has to absorb.

Test and evaluation

Measurement procedures, repeatability, and traceability for marine unmanned systems.
